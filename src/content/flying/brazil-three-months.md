The sabbatical started in Brazil and stayed there for three months. I came back
in May. Some of it was flying, and a surprising amount of it was not, which is
the part I did not plan for and would not trade.

## The flying

Three weeks in northern Brazil with a group of friends out of Arizona. Northern
Brazil is the sort of place that recalibrates what you think a normal day looks
like — long, consistent, and generous in a way the western US mostly is not.

I flew the whole trip on the Nova Vortex, which is the wing that carried me
through Brazil and the spring before I stepped up in June.

## The rest of it

Most of the three months was based in Santa Catarina, in the south, and that is
where the trip stopped being a flying trip and started being something better.

I met my girlfriend's family. I learned to kiteboard, which is a genuinely
humbling thing to do as someone who already thinks he understands wind — the
sport punishes a completely different set of mistakes than paragliding does, and
the water does not care what your airtime is. Ibiraquera turned out to be the
right place to be bad at it.

Beyond that: long hikes along the southern coastline, more all-you-can-eat
rodízio than I will admit to in writing, and cities like Balneário Camboriú that
are worth the trip on their own.

I studied Portuguese the whole time and I am still not good at it. I can hold a
conversation about basic things. That is a real improvement over where I started
and nowhere near where I want to be.

## Going back

I will definitely visit again. Ideally with better Portuguese and a season's
worth more flying behind me.
