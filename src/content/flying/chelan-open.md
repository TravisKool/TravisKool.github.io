The Ozone Chelan Open — a C-wing-and-below sports class event, 140 pilots, and a
much more competitive field than "sports class" makes it sound.

## Conditions

Chelan flatland flying in June. The thermals were very strong, up to eight
metres a second, over the Washington desert. On the ground it was fairly hot;
in the sky the temperature was perfect, which is a combination I would take
every week of the year.

We had good weather for the whole event, which is not something you can count on
and is the difference between a competition and a week of waiting.

## The flight

This was my second competition ever. I landed top ten on one of the tasks and
finished top twenty overall — both firsts for me, and on a wing I had owned for
about a week.

Eight metres a second is enough to change how you fly a task. You stop
optimising for finding lift and start optimising for how quickly you can get
back on glide, because the climbs will be there and the clock will not wait.

## Afterwards

This is my new favourite competition. Great conditions, a strong field, and the
first time a result made me think the standings were something I could actually
work on rather than just something that happened to me.
