The Willi XC is a different animal from the competitions either side of it. It
is an XC-style competition — much more casual, and not official the way the
Chelan and Golden comps were. There is no task. You fly every day you can, in whatever
direction the day is offering, and the day decides what it is worth.

Which is how the best flight I have ever had happened on a week where nobody
called a task.

## The flight

Launched Mt 7 at 1:15 PM and landed seven hours and seven minutes later. 184 km
of free distance, scoring as a 177 km flat triangle, 249.2 points — my best
distance, my best duration and my best score, all in one flight.

For most of the way out I was sharing the sky with two other pilots I did not
know. We flew a long way together and I only met them properly on the ground
afterwards, which is a normal and slightly absurd feature of this sport.

## The turn

The decision the flight actually turned on was when to come back.

I chose to turn around and fly home with a different pilot, and I am glad I
turned when I did — the flight was already long by then, and every extra
kilometre out is a kilometre you have to earn twice. Turning early enough that
the return is still a flight rather than a rescue is most of the skill.

The pilot I ended up flying home with was a local, and he went on to win the
competition. On the way back he talked us into pushing past the point where we
had launched, to pick up some extra distance rather than take the easy line to
the LZ.

I was fairly sure we were going to land out. We did not. We made it all the way
back to the LZ, after a flight that was already the longest of my life.

## Decisions I would change

Nothing about the turn. Everything I would change is about trusting the local
sooner — my read on that last push was that it was not on, and it was on, and
the person telling me so had a season of that valley behind him and won the
whole thing a few days later.

The best call I made all day was flying next to people who knew more than me.
